# RITZZ QA Workflow

Every project records stage QA history at `projects/<project>/qa/qa_report.json`. Each stage entry has `PASS`, `REVIEW`, or `FAIL`, checks, findings, recommendations, reviewer, and attempt number. Project status is aggregated from the latest attempt for each stage; a passing correction can clear an earlier failure while preserving its history.

## Always-on checks

- Research: source-reference coverage, claim confidence, and possible contradictions. A `FAIL` or `REVIEW` triggers one forced regeneration with the exact findings and recommendations; outlining proceeds only after `PASS`.
- Outline: section-duration sum and configured duration band. A failed check triggers one forced regeneration with the failed checks as guidance; script generation proceeds only after `PASS`.
- Script: the configurable `RITZZ_ANCIENT_HUMAN_CURIOSITY` profile steers an evidence-led narrative arc without forcing modern analogies or a historical frame where unsupported. The artifact retains a structured hook plan and one question/evidence/reveal/next-question/visual opportunity movement per outline section. Structured QA checks hook-to-investigation continuity, open loops, evolving questions, evidence integration, uncertainty, relevance, payoff, visualizability, fact-listing, and originality against approved research and outline inputs. Actionable findings revise only identified sections, once, and save review history to `script/script_quality_review.json` and `qa/qa_report.json`; revised evidence IDs are constrained to each outline section's approved sources, and the revised script is reviewed again. This is an automated safeguard, not proof of factual accuracy. The separate hook review compares three to five meaningfully distinct candidates, preferring a concrete modern problem and its familiar solution connected to the same ancient problem when the research supports that bridge. It requires an early unanswered question, an open loop, supported source IDs, and a configured weighted quality score; the spoken opening is 20–35 seconds at the configured narration pace. Weak hooks receive up to two hook-only regeneration attempts, so the rest of the script is preserved. Its report is saved as `script/hook_evaluation.json`. Generation and QA use `ProductionConfig` pacing, hook score weights, and target/minimum/maximum duration settings.
- Packaging: required title, description, and tags. Invalid metadata drafts are regenerated once with their validation errors included as correction guidance.
- Voice: measured minimum duration, character alignment, and actual timestamped opening-hook duration when generated into a project audio directory. The spoken hook must be the first narration text and last 20–35 seconds; an out-of-range or mismatched hook triggers one hook-only repair from approved research and replacement narration, then fails the voice stage and blocks storyboard/rendering if still invalid. Too-short overall audio prompts one full script regeneration from the saved research and outline, then one replacement narration. Invalid character alignment triggers one narration retry. Audio is never stretched; if the bounded repair still fails, scene timing is blocked.
- Audio-timed storyboard: continuous timeline, scene holds between 3 and 4 seconds, complete-word boundaries, static camera, hard cuts, and text-free image prompts. The grouping prefers sentence/clause ends, pauses, meaningful visual changes, and durations near 3.5 seconds. If actual aligned word boundaries cannot represent continuous coverage within the configured bounds, the stage fails explicitly for review rather than creating an undersized scene. The workflow derives a research-grounded `visual_world_bible.json` and matching `scene_visual_contracts.json`; contracts record purpose, subject/action/environment, ambiguity resolution, required/prohibited objects, and continuity cues. Both inform image prompts and semantic review; missing or mismatched contracts fail deterministic preflight.
- Render: image validity, scene order, gaps/overlaps, duration consistency, video/audio streams, and timeline coverage.
- Production pipeline image review: before rendering, deterministic checks validate image readability, configured dimensions, storyboard structure and narration-alignment coverage, and perceptually similar nearby images. OpenAI Vision reviews scene/narration relevance, visual intent, historical/world consistency, required/forbidden objects, and continuity; it does not score text or callouts. Reviews use structured, size-bounded batches with configurable bounded concurrency and retries; only repaired scenes are reviewed again. Passing reviews are cached by image and relevant scene/world inputs. A clear `FAIL`, or an actionable `REVIEW`, can trigger a targeted image repair using a scene-specific correction that preserves character, style, and visual-contract intent. Uncertain cases without a concrete correction preserve the original image and remain visible for human review.
- Thumbnail packaging: after final metadata, the workflow generates exactly three topic-grounded concepts with distinct curiosity angles. Hook validation measures uppercase glyph width, not just word count. A trusted human selects one before artwork generation. The artwork is generated without text, then the approved uppercase hook is composed deterministically as one large, high-contrast line on a 1280×720 image, with a black outline, adaptive sizing, sampled-background color choice, and safe margins. Structured visual review checks topic/world accuracy, title pairing, curiosity, fit, style, focal clarity, clutter, and mobile readability.

## Bounded automatic correction

Production image review and deterministic technical render QA allow up to three repair attempts. Other stages retain the retry limits described above. Initial findings and each verification result are retained in QA history; only a verified `PASS` completes the QA stage and allows dependent work to proceed. If QA is still not `PASS` after the stage's bounded retries, that stage remains incomplete and the workflow stops with its findings retained for diagnosis.

The production render stage checks generated images against their narration, visual description, and applicable visual-world contracts. Video-frame editorial text is not a QA criterion and is not generated or repaired. Technical render QA validates each scene hold against the configured 3–4-second bounds, along with image readability, scene order, gaps/overlaps, timestamp coverage, scene-plan/audio duration, rendered audio/video streams, loudness, and audio/video duration consistency.

`PilotVideoQA.run_rendered_video_semantic` is available for explicit rendered-frame review, but is not currently wired as an automatic production stage. The production workflow uses the bounded pre-render image review and deterministic post-render technical QA. Neither check replaces listening to the complete audio or watching the video.

## Current limits

- AI reviews are advisory judgments, not proof that facts or images are correct. Visual ambiguity, uncertain facts, and human approval are not auto-marked `PASS`; unresolved review findings stay visible and block dependent stages and approval/publication.
- Voice QA is deterministic duration/alignment validation; it does not use an AI audio-quality reviewer.
- Deterministic render QA runs after video generation. It checks image readability, scene order, gaps/overlaps, scene duration bounds, timestamp coverage, scene-plan/audio duration, rendered audio/video streams, loudness, and rendered-video/audio duration (within 0.25 seconds). Integrated loudness passes within ±2 LU of the configured −14 LUFS target, and true peak must remain at or below the configured ceiling plus 0.1 dB measurement tolerance. Any technical `FAIL` or `REVIEW` triggers another render; synchronization-related findings additionally rebuild the synchronized and motion plans before rerendering.
- The separate `run_pilot_audio_image_match.py` check compares each current audio-timed scene image with that scene's narration and timestamp range. It is an AI relevance check, not waveform alignment; the deterministic post-render QA is the audio/video synchronization check.
- The optional content CLI AI review remains opt-in. Production rendering enables image/narration review automatically.
- Human approval, uncertain factual claims, and visual ambiguity without a concrete correction remain human decisions. The system does not silently convert those findings to `PASS`.
