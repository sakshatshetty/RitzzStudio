# RITZZ QA Workflow

Every project records stage QA history at `projects/<project>/qa/qa_report.json`. Each stage entry has `PASS`, `REVIEW`, or `FAIL`, checks, findings, recommendations, reviewer, and attempt number. Project status is aggregated from the latest attempt for each stage; a passing correction can clear an earlier failure while preserving its history.

## Always-on checks

- Research: source-reference coverage, claim confidence, and possible contradictions. A `FAIL` or `REVIEW` triggers one forced regeneration with the exact findings and recommendations; outlining proceeds only after `PASS`.
- Outline: section-duration sum and configured duration band. A failed check triggers one forced regeneration with the failed checks as guidance; script generation proceeds only after `PASS`.
- Script: structure, research/outline topic alignment, word count, duration, and the opening hook's match to the spoken first section and approximate 10-second length. A separate structured hook review scores curiosity, tension, specificity, stakes, novelty, clarity, open loop, payoff promise, and factual support. Alternatives must be 13–38 words and cite approved research source IDs; only the hook prefix is replaced, and the report is saved as `script/hook_evaluation.json`. A `FAIL` or `REVIEW` triggers one forced regeneration with the exact findings; voice generation proceeds only after `PASS`.
- Packaging: required title, description, and tags. Invalid metadata drafts are regenerated once with their validation errors included as correction guidance.
- Voice: measured minimum duration and character alignment when generated into a project audio directory. Too-short audio prompts one script regeneration from the saved research and outline, then one replacement narration. Invalid character alignment triggers one narration retry. Audio is never stretched; if the bounded repair still fails, scene timing is blocked.
- Audio-timed storyboard: continuous timeline, prompts, static camera, hard cuts, and an explicit editorial decision for every grouped scene. After timing and editorial planning, the workflow derives a research-grounded `visual_world_bible.json` and a matching `scene_visual_contracts.json`; each contract records scene purpose, subject/action/environment, ambiguity resolution, required/prohibited objects, and continuity cues. Both are included in image prompts and semantic review, and a project with a bible but missing/mismatched per-scene contracts fails deterministic preflight. QA applies safe deterministic repairs to camera/cut settings, missing prompts, recoverable timeline continuity, and editorial callout planning, then reruns the checks. An unresolved issue blocks image generation. The planner targets one meaningful, one-word uppercase callout about every 3–4 scenes, but marks a scene `callout_not_warranted` with a reason when no editorial emphasis is justified. QA distinguishes valid, intentionally skipped, unexpectedly missing, and malformed callouts; it reports scene coverage, average callout interval, and longest interval. When assigned, the exact approved uppercase word is included in the image-generation prompt and rendered as part of the generated illustration; it is not added later as an FFmpeg text overlay.
- Render: image validity, scene order, gaps/overlaps, duration consistency, video/audio streams, and timeline coverage.
- Production pipeline image review: before rendering, deterministic checks validate image readability, configured dimensions, storyboard structure and narration-alignment coverage, and detect perceptually similar nearby images. OpenAI Vision then checks generated images against the complete scene narration, visual description, project visual world, scene contract, and assigned editorial word. Structured results categorize clear issues (for example anachronism, ambiguity, wrong action/environment, missing/forbidden objects, continuity, and editorial mismatch). Reviews use structured, size-bounded batches with configurable bounded concurrency and retries; only repaired scenes are reviewed again. Passing semantic reviews are cached by image and all relevant scene/world inputs so changed contracts or constraints invalidate stale approvals. A `FAIL` records a proposed fix in the QA report and triggers a targeted image repair, using the reviewer correction and category-specific repair guidance. Per-scene findings, prompts, category, and verification are appended to `qa/visual_repair_history.json`; an identical prior repair prompt is not blindly repeated. A `REVIEW` receives a repair only when the reviewer supplies a concrete correction; uncertain cases without one preserve the original image, remain `REVIEW`, and block rendering rather than guessing or auto-approving.

## Bounded automatic correction

Production image review and deterministic technical render QA allow up to three repair attempts. Other stages retain the retry limits described above. Initial findings and each verification result are retained in QA history; only a verified `PASS` completes the QA stage and allows dependent work to proceed. If QA is still not `PASS` after the stage's bounded retries, that stage remains incomplete and the workflow stops with its findings retained for diagnosis.

The production render stage checks generated images against their narration and visual description, and verifies that an assigned editorial word fits the scene and appears in the generated illustration. A clear `FAIL` includes a proposed fix in the QA report and triggers targeted image regeneration; a `REVIEW` is repaired only when the reviewer supplies a concrete correction. An editorial word may move to an adjacent scene only when spacing is retained. Original images are retained under `qa/image_repair/`, every finding and repair verification is recorded in QA history, the updated storyboard and image manifest are saved, and changed scenes are reviewed again. If uncertainty has no safe, actionable correction, the finding stays `REVIEW` and blocks rendering for a human decision. On a failed GitHub render, the workflow preserves the QA report as a diagnostics artifact so a reviewer can identify the scene, rationale, and proposed fix. The current rerun workflow reuses the saved project artifact; it does not provide an upload/approval control for a human-edited replacement image. Applying a manual replacement to a GitHub rerun therefore requires adding that artifact-input path or running the corrected project outside the current workflow.

`PilotVideoQA.run_rendered_video_semantic` is available for explicit rendered-frame review, but is not currently wired as an automatic production stage. The production workflow uses the bounded pre-render image review and deterministic post-render technical QA. Neither check replaces listening to the complete audio or watching the video.

## Current limits

- AI reviews are advisory judgments, not proof that facts or images are correct. Editorial ambiguity, uncertain facts, and human approval are not auto-marked `PASS`; unresolved review findings stay visible and block dependent stages and approval/publication.
- Voice QA is deterministic duration/alignment validation; it does not use an AI audio-quality reviewer.
- Deterministic render QA runs after video generation. It checks image readability, scene order, gaps/overlaps, timestamp coverage, scene-plan/audio duration, rendered audio/video streams, loudness, editorial callouts, and rendered-video/audio duration (within 0.25 seconds). Integrated loudness passes within ±2 LU of the configured −14 LUFS target, and true peak must remain at or below the configured ceiling plus 0.1 dB measurement tolerance. Any technical `FAIL` or `REVIEW` triggers another render; synchronization-related findings additionally rebuild the synchronized and motion plans before rerendering. Measured loudness/peak findings adjust the next render's FFmpeg normalization targets rather than repeating the same filter. The pipeline records each attempt and marks render/technical QA complete only after a `PASS`. After three unsuccessful attempts, the technical QA stage remains failed and the artifact is retained for diagnosis.
- The separate `run_pilot_audio_image_match.py` check compares each current audio-timed scene image with that scene's narration and timestamp range. It is an AI relevance check, not waveform alignment; the deterministic post-render QA is the audio/video synchronization check.
- The optional content CLI AI review remains opt-in. Production rendering enables image/narration review automatically.
- Human approval, uncertain factual claims, and visual ambiguity without a concrete correction remain human decisions. The system does not silently convert those findings to `PASS`.
